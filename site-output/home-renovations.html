What a full renovation actually involves

A whole-house renovation is not one big job. It is a dozen jobs that have to happen in the right order, and the order is what separates a smooth project from a house that is open for months. Structural work before services. Services before closing walls. Waterproofing before finishes. Finishes before the snag list.

Cape Town's housing stock makes this more interesting than it sounds. A 1950s Goodwood house has solid brick walls, no cavity and a damp course that may or may not still work. A 1970s Bellville house may have been extended twice, each time with a roof tied into the last one. A newer Durbanville home has modern services but detailing at parapets and balconies that was value-engineered at construction.

We plan around the structure you actually have. Sometimes that means keeping a wall that a plan said to remove, because it is carrying more than the drawing showed. Sometimes it means opening a room differently to avoid moving a drain. We will always tell you which is which, and what each option costs, before demolition starts.

What we take on

Anything from a single room to a full refurbishment, with the trades sequenced by one team.

Open-plan changes

Walls removed or opened, with the structural support designed and installed properly, not assumed.

Structural openings

Lintels and beams sized and fitted, temporary support during the work, and the finish made good.

Kitchens and bathrooms

Both handled as part of the same programme so the plumbing and electrical work happens once.

Flooring and ceilings

Screed repair, tiling, timber and laminate, plus ceilings and cornices matched to the house.

Doors and windows

Replacement, new openings, and the flashing and sealing details that keep water out.

Full refurbishment

Strip to structure, rewire, re-plumb, replaster, waterproof and finish, phased so the house stays usable.

How a renovation runs

1. Survey

Every room measured, services checked, roof and damp assessed, and priorities agreed with you.

2. Scope and sequence

A written scope split into essential and optional, with the order of work and who does what.

3. Structure and first fix

Openings formed, walls removed with support, plumbing and electrical roughed in.

4. Close up and finish

Plaster, waterproofing, tiling, joinery, paint and the fittings, in the planned order.

5. Snag and hand over

A joint walk-through, a written snag list, and the fixes completed before final payment.
