Older Bellville homes need diagnosis, not a coat of paint

Much of Bellville was built between the 1950s and the 1980s, in solid brick with thick plaster and no cavity. It is a good way to build a house, and it means a wall that gets wet stays wet for a long time. When the damp course at the base fails, the moisture has nowhere to go but up into the plaster, and out through your skirting board.

We see the same three problems in Bellville again and again. Rising damp at ground level, especially on the side that gets no sun. Penetrating damp around windows and under sills where the sealant has aged out. And roof leaks at the junction between the original house and a later addition, which is almost always where a previous builder tied a new roof into an old one without redoing the flashing.

Repainting over any of those buys you one season. We test the moisture, find where it enters, and treat the cause before we quote the cosmetic work.
