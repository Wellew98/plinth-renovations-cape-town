01The area

Brackenfell, as we find it

Brackenfell grew mostly from the 1970s onward, and the housing shows it: double-storey family homes, large plots, plenty of room to extend, and boundary walls running the length of every street. The newer estates on the northern edge bring their own rules about what may be built.

What makes work here different is exposure. There is little to break the north-westerly wind, so rain arrives at an angle and hits gable ends and side walls that other suburbs never have trouble with. We specify for that rather than for the average Cape Town house.

03What fails here

The Brackenfell problems we get called for

These come up more often in this area than in the city as a whole.

  • Wind-driven rain pushes water sideways into gable ends and around window frames.
  • Long boundary walls crack at the piers and lean after years of wind load.
  • Damp appears on the windward wall where the plaster was never sealed.
