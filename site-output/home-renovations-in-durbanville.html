Durbanville homes, and what the weather does to them

Durbanville sits high and open on the northern edge of the city, which is good for views and hard on walls. The north-westerly front that comes through in winter drives rain almost horizontally into west and north-facing facades, and any crack in the plaster or gap around a window becomes an entry point rather than a cosmetic problem.

The older houses near the town centre were built with plaster on brick, often with a damp course that has since failed. That shows up as rising damp at skirting level, paint that bubbles back within a season, and a smell in the rooms that face the ground. Newer estates have the opposite problem: the waterproofing is recent but the detailing at parapets, balconies and roof junctions is where it leaks.

We work on both. That includes single-storey family homes in Vierlanden and Goedemoed, townhouse complexes where the body corporate needs a scope it can approve, and older properties where the renovation has to respect a structure worth keeping.
