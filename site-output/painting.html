Why repaints fail in one season

A coat of paint over a damp wall will blister. Paint over chalky, powdery plaster will peel in sheets. Paint over a crack that is still moving will crack again, in the same line, within months. None of these are paint problems. They are preparation problems, and Cape Town's coastal air and winter rain find every one of them.

We prepare before we paint. That means washing and degreasing, scraping back anything loose, cutting out cracks and filling them with something that can move, sealing bare plaster with the right primer, and only then applying a system matched to the surface. On exterior work it also means waiting for the wall to be dry, which in winter can mean rescheduling a day rather than painting wet masonry and hoping.

We also do the work that sits between painting and building: repairing blown plaster, replacing rotten window beads, re-puttying glass, sealing joints that have opened, and coating roofs that are sound but have lost their protection.

What we paint and prep

The surface decides the system. Enamel on a wall that needs a breathable coating is a mistake that shows up in the first wet winter.

Interior walls and ceilings

Crack repair, skimming where needed, primer and two coats, with sheeting and daily clean-up.

Exterior walls

Crack cutting, plaster repair, fungicidal wash and a coating chosen for the exposure.

Roof coating

Cleaning, priming and a reflective or protective coating where the roof is sound but unprotected.

Kitchen cabinets

Doors and frames removed, degreased, sanded, primed and sprayed or brushed, then rehung.

Doors, windows and trim

Sanding back, filling, priming and enamel, including the bottom edge that weathers first.

Damp-affected walls

Only after the source is fixed. Painting a damp wall is a delay, not a repair.

How we work

1. Assess the surface

Sound, chalky, damp or previously coated with something that will not accept new paint.

2. Prepare

Wash, scrape, cut out cracks, repair plaster, sand, and mask everything that is staying.

3. Prime

The right primer for bare plaster, masonry, timber or a glossy existing coat.

4. Apply

Two coats in the specified system, with the finish checked in daylight rather than under a work light.

5. Inspect together

We walk the job with you, mark anything that needs attention and fix it before we pack up.
