Painting and surface preparation, Cape Town

Painting contractors in Cape Town

Paint is the last five percent of a painting job. The other ninety-five is preparation, and it is where most repaints fail.

01Painting

Why repaints fail in one season

A coat of paint over a damp wall will blister. Paint over chalky, powdery plaster will peel in sheets. Paint over a crack that is still moving will crack again, in the same line, within months. None of these are paint problems. They are preparation problems, and Cape Town's coastal air and winter rain find every one of them. We prepare before we paint. That means washing and degreasing, scraping back anything loose, cutting out cracks and filling them with something that can move, sealing bare plaster with the right primer, and only then applying a system matched to the surface. On exterior work it also means waiting for the wall to be dry, which in winter can mean rescheduling a day rather than painting wet masonry and hoping. We also do the work that sits between painting and building: repairing blown plaster, replacing rotten window beads, re-puttying glass, sealing joints that have opened, and coating roofs that are sound but have lost their protection.

04How It Works

How we work

1

1. Assess the surface

Sound, chalky, damp or previously coated with something that will not accept new paint.

2

2. Prepare

Wash, scrape, cut out cracks, repair plaster, sand, and mask everything that is staying.

3

3. Prime

The right primer for bare plaster, masonry, timber or a glossy existing coat.

4

4. Apply

Two coats in the specified system, with the finish checked in daylight rather than under a work light.

5

5. Inspect together

We walk the job with you, mark anything that needs attention and fix it before we pack up.

  • Preparation is the job: We would rather spend an extra day prepping than come back next winter to fix peeling paint.
  • We repair before we paint: Blown plaster, rotten beads and open joints are fixed, not filled and hidden.
  • Weathered in, not painted over: Exterior work is scheduled around dry walls, not around the calendar.
  • Neat in a lived-in house: Furniture covered, floors protected, and a clean room at the end of each day.
